San Bartolo Coyotepec, Oaxaca

San Bartolo Coyotepec, home of Oaxaca's black pottery, lies about half an hour south of the state capital. It suits movers who want village quiet, a workshop culture, and the city's hospitals, banks, and migration office within a short drive.

Guides describe national processes from official sources and each town's practical layer from a public web pass. Cost figures wait for a Market Watch sweep; we show what we know and nothing invented.

Visa path

Mexico grants temporary residency at a consulate before you move: you present financial solvency, a job offer, or a family tie, receive the visa in your passport, then exchange it for a residency card at an Instituto Nacional de Migración office in Mexico. Permanent residency works the same way with higher thresholds. Figures and rules change, so read the current requirements before you apply.

Handle migration paperwork at the INM delegation in Oaxaca city, about 30 minutes north on the highway. Same day round trips are normal for residents here.

Source: Instituto Nacional de Migración (INM)

Healthcare

Public coverage runs through IMSS, which residents join through employment or a voluntary scheme, and many long stayers pair it with private consultorios that cost a fraction of US prices. Bring prescriptions in their original boxes, get a checkup before the move, and decide your insurance mix while you are healthy rather than after.

The town has a health center and private consultorios. Oaxaca city holds the hospitals and specialists, close enough for routine appointments without moving house.

Source: IMSS, the public health insurer

Banking

With a residency card, a CURP, and proof of address, national banks open accounts, and fintech apps open faster and are accepted nearly everywhere. SPEI transfers move money between accounts in seconds, and cash still runs markets and small shops. Expect the first account to take an in person visit, and keep documents from your visa path handy.

ATMs stand on the main road and workshops sell cash and transfer alike. Branch banks stay in Oaxaca city; a national account plus a fintech app covers most needs.

Internet

The fixed market is led by Telmex, izzi, Totalplay, and Axtel, with AT&T and Telcel at the front of mobile, all under the watch of the Instituto Federal de Telecomunicaciones. Coverage is strong on main streets, thinner at town edges, and satellite fills remote casitas. The honest method is the same everywhere: test with your own devices at the exact address before you sign.

Main road fixed coverage is solid and side streets vary; the village sits low, so signal is friendlier than in the sierra towns.

Source: Instituto Federal de Telecomunicaciones (IFT)

Cost of a month

Real monthly figures are published only after a sweep measures them, so no town guide here invents a number. The honest frame: rents move with season and street, small town baskets run below Mexico City, INEGI publishes the national price statistics, and the variables that matter are housing, transport, and how often you eat out.

Proximity to Oaxaca city keeps goods cheap and rents a notch above remote villages. Real figures follow the first sweep.

Source: INEGI national statistics
