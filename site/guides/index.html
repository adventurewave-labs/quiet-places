Relocation guides

Practical guides per town: the visa path, healthcare, banking, internet, and the real cost of a month, bilingual and refreshed every quarter.

Guides describe national processes from official sources and each town's practical layer from a public web pass. Cost figures wait for a Market Watch sweep; we show what we know and nothing invented.

  • Tepoztlán Morelos, México Tepoztlán sits under its cliffs about an hour south of Mexico City, which makes it the easiest quiet town to reach from the capital. Moving here means handling paperwork in Cuernavaca, budgeting for a weekend tourist rhythm, and choosing between the town center and the outlying colonias.
  • Bacalar Quintana Roo, México Bacalar is a small lagoon town in southern Quintana Roo, far from the Riviera Maya's pace. Moving here trades big city services for the water at your door; Chetumal, the state capital, covers the paperwork, the hospitals, and the airport about 40 minutes south.
  • Real de Catorce San Luis Potosí, México Real de Catorce is a high desert former silver town reached through a tunnel, with Matehuala as its service city on the plain below. Moving here is a commitment: cold nights, cobblestones, one road in, and services that live 30 to 45 minutes downslope.
  • Teotitlán del Valle Oaxaca, México Teotitlán del Valle is a Zapotec weaving village half an hour from Oaxaca city, which puts full city services within a short colectivo ride while the village keeps its own rhythm of looms, markets, and saints days. Moving here means village rent below city rates, a craft economy around you, and paperwork and hospitals a short ride away.
  • San Bartolo Coyotepec Oaxaca, México San Bartolo Coyotepec, home of Oaxaca's black pottery, lies about half an hour south of the state capital. It suits movers who want village quiet, a workshop culture, and the city's hospitals, banks, and migration office within a short drive.
